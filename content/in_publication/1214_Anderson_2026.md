abstract: 'The genus <i>Coelosternus</i> Sahlberg, 1823 (Coleoptera: Curculionidae: Cryptorhynchinae) is redefined as a circum-Caribbean genus characterized by small, connate tarsal claws, a visible scutellar shield and association with plants in the subfamily Theophrastoideae of the Primulaceae, based on the correct identification of <i>Coelosternus balteatus</i> Sahlberg, 1823, the type species of the genus. Six new species of <i>Coelosternus</i> are described: <i>C. jacquiniae</i> Anderson, new species (Florida and the Bahamas); <i>C. alamedai</i> Anderson, new species (Cuba); <i>C. impressiventris</i> Anderson, new species (Cuba), <i>C. grandis</i> Anderson, new species (Cuba), <i>C. ivieorum</i> Anderson, new species (Dominican Republic) and, <i>C. magnificus</i> Anderson, new species (Mexico and Guatemala). <i>Coelosternus balteatus</i> is redescribed and reported from the British Virgin Islands, Guadeloupe, Montserrat, Puerto Rico, Saint Barthélemy, St. Lucia and U.S. Virgin Islands. Specimens of <i>C. balteatus</i> from Puerto Rico are associated with <i>Jacquinia arborea</i> Vahl; <i>C. jacquiniae</i> develops in the berries of <i>Jacquinia keyensis</i> Mez; <i>C. alamedai</i> is associated with <i>Bonellia stenophylla</i> (Urb.) B.Ståhl and Källersjö; and <i>C. magnificus</i> has been intercepted at U.S. ports in a seed of an undetermined species of <i>Jacquinia</i> and on “duruche”, <i>Bonellia macrocarpa</i> (Cav.) B.Ståhl and Källersjö. Diagnostic characters and images of habitus and male genitalia for each species are presented and discussed. <i>Graphonotus variisquamis</i> Champion, 1906 is moved to <i>Bothrobatys</i> Schönherr as <i>Bothrobatys variisquamis</i> (Champion, 1906), new combination. The junior homonym <i>Graphonotus</i> Chevrolat, 1880 (not Agassiz, 1846) (type species <i>Cryptorhynchus albocaudatus</i> Rosenschoeld) is removed from synonymy with <i>Coelosternus</i> and the replacement name <i>Graphonotatus</i> Anderson, new replacement name is proposed. Resulting nomenclatural changes are discussed.'
