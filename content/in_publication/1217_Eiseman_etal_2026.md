abstract: 'The species of Gracillariidae feeding on Juglandaceae in North America are reviewed, including host, distribution, and parasitoid records. <i>Caloptilia blandella</i> (Clemens) feeds on <i>Juglans</i> spp. (walnut and butternut) and is likely responsible for occasional mines found on <i>Carya</i> spp. (hickory and pecan). <i>Caloptilia juglandiella</i> (Chambers), new synonym, is shown to be a dark overwintering form of <i>C. blandella</i>. <i>Cameraria caryaefoliella</i> (Clemens) feeds on <i>Carya</i> and <i>Juglans</i> in the east, and mines of this or another, undescribed <i>Cameraria</i> species occur on <i>Juglans</i> in the western USA. <i>Cameraria eppelsheimii</i> (Frey and Boll), known from a single specimen purportedly reared from <i>Carya</i> in Texas, is an enigma requiring further study. <i>Phyllonorycter caryaealbella</i> (Chambers) feeds on hickories in <i>Carya</i> section <i>Carya</i>, while the hosts of <i>P. olivaeformis</i> (Braun) are in <i>Carya</i> section <i>Apocarya</i> (pecan and bitternut hickory). Prior records of <i>P. caryaealbella</i> from pecan (<i>C. illinoinensis</i> (Wangenh.) K. Koch) represent misidentifications of <i>P. olivaeformis</i>. DNA barcodes, genitalia, and other distinguishing features of these and other North American Gracillariidae with similar wing patterns are discussed. Mines attributed to the polyphagous species <i>Marmara gulosa</i> Guillén and Davis have been found on stems and fruits of cultivated walnut in southern California, and possible <i>Marmara</i> mines have been found on stems of hickory and walnut in eastern North America. Linear leaf mines of an unknown gracillariid have been found on black hickory (<i>Carya texana</i> Buckley) in Oklahoma.'
