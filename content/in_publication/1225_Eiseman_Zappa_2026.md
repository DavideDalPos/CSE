abstract: 'The North American species of Gracillariidae feeding on <i>Tilia</i> L. (Malvaceae) are reviewed. Three species of <i>Phyllonorycter</i> Hübner mine leaves of basswood (<i>T. americana</i> L.) in its native range (east of the Rocky Mountains), each forming a distinctly different mine and differing in adult coloration: <i>P. lucetiella</i> (Clemens), <i>P. tiliacella</i> (Chambers), and <i>P. tiliivorus</i> Eiseman and Zappa, new species. The male and female genitalia of each species are illustrated for the first time, the pupal cremasters are compared, and data concerning phenology, geographic distribution, parasitoids, and DNA barcoding are summarized. An undescribed species of <i>Marmara</i> Clemens mining in <i>Tilia</i> stems is known from numerous observations of mines in eastern Canada and the northern USA, along with a single larva from which a DNA barcode was obtained. Records of <i>Marmara</i> from other malvaceous hosts are also summarized.'
