abstract: 'The North American species of <i>Rhopobota</i> Lederer (Lepidoptera: Tortricidae: Olethreutinae: Eucosmini) are reviewed. All feed as larvae on hollies (Aquifoliaceae: <i>Ilex</i> Tourn. ex L.). The Holarctic species <i>R. naevana</i> (Hübner), which feeds on evergreen and deciduous hollies as well as ericaceous and other plants, overwinters as eggs and apparently never feeds as a leafminer. <i>Rhopobota dietziana</i> (Kearfott) s. str. is an external leaf feeder on various deciduous and evergreen hollies, overwintering as pupae, while <i>R. dietziana glabricola</i> Eiseman, new subspecies, was reared from larvae found mining leaves of inkberry (<i>I. glabra</i> (L.) A. Gray) in late March. <i>Rhopobota finitimana</i> (Heinrich) feeds initially as a leafminer, and later as a leaftier, on deciduous and evergreen hollies; it mostly overwinters as pupae, but on North Carolina’s coastal plain it overwinters as leaf-tying larvae on yaupon (<i>I. vomitoria</i> Aiton). <i>Rhopobota opacae</i> Eiseman, new species, has a larval biology similar to that of <i>R. finitimana</i> but is a specialist on American holly (<i>I. opaca</i> Aiton) and overwinters as leaf-mining larvae. This species previously has been confused with <i>R. finitimana</i> but differs substantially in its DNA barcode and female genitalia; the male differs more subtly in its genitalia, but can be distinguished externally by the location of the androconial scales. DNA barcodes of the other species are also discussed.'
