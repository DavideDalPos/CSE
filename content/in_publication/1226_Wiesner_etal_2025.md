abstract: 'We demonstrate how “Cybertaxonomy”, a remote, digital workflow, facilitates taxonomic research in countries with strict export regulations on biological material. By dividing labor between trained, on-site personnel in the country of origin and external taxonomic specialists, this model ensures that physical specimens remain locally. On-site collaborators provide initial overview photographs and collection data, allowing specialists to request targeted high-resolution images, specific anatomical preparations, or precise measurements. This digital framework applies equally to fresh field material and legacy museum collections. The downstream workflow categorizes specimens into three streams: known species, new locality records, and potentially novel species. We illustrate this approach through two published case studies on tiger beetles (Coleoptera: Cicindelidae): the description of a new species with material gathered from several Brazilian museum collections, and the documentation of new geographic records in India via photographic observation. In both instances, remote taxonomic assessment was seamlessly integrated with documentation guidance and manuscript preparation, eliminating the need for international specimen shipment.'
