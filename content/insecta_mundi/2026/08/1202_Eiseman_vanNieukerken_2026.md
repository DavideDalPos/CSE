abstract: 'Species of <i>Phyllocnistis</i> Zeller (Lepidoptera: Gracillariidae) mining leaves of plants in the family Vitaceae are reviewed worldwide, including descriptions and illustrations of adults and leaf mines. Host plant and parasitoid records, distribution data, and DNA barcoding data are summarized for each species, with many new records provided. Four species are native to North America: <i>P. vitegenella</i> Clemens is transcontinental, but rare in the West; <i>P. vitifoliella</i> Chambers and <i>P. ampelopsiella</i> Chambers are strictly eastern; and <i>P. occidentalis</i> Eiseman and van Nieukerken, new species, is strictly western. Three of these—<i>P. vitegenella</i>, <i>P. vitifoliella</i>, and <i>P. occidentalis</i>—are commonly found on <i>Vitis</i> L. They have been confused in recent decades, and due to a mistaken belief that they are synonymous, the species that has been spreading across Europe since the 1990s was misidentifed as <i>P. vitegenella</i>, but it is in fact <i>P. vitifoliella</i>. The four North American species are best distinguished from one another by leaf mine characteristics and DNA barcodes. The four putative Asian species are <i>P. toparcha</i> Meyrick (India, Japan), <i>P. temperatior</i> Meyrick (Indonesia), <i>P. vitella</i> Ermolaev (Russian Far East), and an unnamed species on <i>Ampelopsis</i> Michx. (Japan, Taiwan). More DNA barcoding data will be needed to clarify their statuses. Leaf mines found on <i>Rhoicissus</i> Planch. in South Africa and on <i>Cissus</i> L. in Gabon indicate that at least two undescribed species are present in Africa. Another undescribed species has been reared from <i>Apocissus</i> Jackes and Trias-Blasi in Australia.'
